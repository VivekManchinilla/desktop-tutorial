About Ratan Tata

Born on 28 December 1937 in Bombay (now Mumbai), Ratan Naval Tata is an Indian industrialist and philanthropist. He served as Chairman of Tata Sons and led the Tata Group through a period of global expansion, acquisitions, and innovation. Educated in architecture at Cornell University (B.Arch, 1962) and later an alumnus of Harvard Business School’s Advanced Management Program, he is known for his belief in “doing the right thing even when no one is watching.”

Leadership

As Chairman (1991–2012, interim 2016–2017), he oversaw transformative acquisitions like Tetley, Corus, and Jaguar Land Rover, while fostering home‑grown innovations such as the Tata Indica and the frugal engineering behind the Tata Nano.

Values

Tata’s leadership emphasized ethics, long‑term thinking, trust with stakeholders, and building institutions that outlive individuals. Much of Tata Sons’ equity is held by philanthropic trusts, channeling profits back into society.

Advisory & Global Roles

He has served on numerous boards and advisory councils across business and academia, and is a trusted voice on entrepreneurship, innovation, and responsible capitalism.

Philanthropy & Social Impact

Through the Tata Trusts and personal initiatives, Ratan Tata has been closely associated with giving in health, education, water, livelihoods, and research. A significant share of Tata Sons is held by charitable trusts, funneling dividends to social causes.

Healthcare

Support for cancer care, public health infrastructure, and medical research, including centers of excellence and community programs.

Education

Scholarships and institutional grants to leading universities in India and abroad; backing for science, engineering, and design education.

R&D & Innovation

Grants for frontier research in technology and society, and initiatives empowering startups and social entrepreneurs.
